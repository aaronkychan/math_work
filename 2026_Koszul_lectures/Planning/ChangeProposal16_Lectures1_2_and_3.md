## Your Questions

### 1. Monomial relations of length 2

You are right, and no restriction is needed. The current definition (S3:208–210) says that $R$ is spanned by composable words. Since $R\subset V\otimes_S V$, those words automatically have length 2. So the definition is already correct, but only for quadratic presentations; my remark was pointing out that implicit restriction. Since you want monomial algebras of any degree, [Replacement 8](#replacement-8) defines monomial ideals generated by composable words of any length (at least 2). It states the basis result in that generality and keeps the quadratic-dual formula for monomial *quadratic* presentations. As a bonus, $\Bbbk A_4/(e_{14})$ from Example `ex:cubic-quotient` becomes an example of a monomial algebra that is not quadratic.

### 2. Should the whole $S$-dual become advanced?

The isomorphism $M^*\cong D(M)$ is natural only *after choosing* $\tau$. Whether that choice is harmless depends on which $S$ the main text allows.

**What the $S$-dual buys in general.**
1. **A canonical $A^!$.** For general semisimple $S$ there is no evident choice-free map $D(W)\otimes_S D(V)\to D(V\otimes_S W)$. The obvious formula $\lambda\otimes\mu\mapsto(v\otimes w\mapsto\mu(v)\lambda(w))$ is not $S$-balanced, since $\mu(vs)\lambda(w)\neq\mu(v)\lambda(sw)$ in general. With $D$ alone, $R^\perp$ and hence $A^!$ must be defined through $\tau$, and independence of $\tau$ becomes an extra lemma.
2. **Later lectures.** The Koszul complex $A\otimes_S{}^*(A^!_i)$, its differential via coevaluation, and the canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet_A(A_0,A_0)\cong(A^!)^{\mathrm{op}}$ are all phrased with $S$-duals in [BGS96, §2.8 and Theorem 2.10.1]. In BGS this last isomorphism is canonical because the $S$-dual is.

**When it buys nothing.** For $S=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n\Bbbk e_i$, the form $\tau(e_i)=1$ is intrinsic, because the $e_i$ are the primitive central idempotents. Then $M^*\cong D(M)$ is canonical, and $D(V\otimes_S W)\cong D(W)\otimes_S D(V)$ is the blockwise pairing $D(e_jWe_\ell)\otimes D(e_iVe_j)$, with no choices. All main-text examples in Lectures 1–3 are of this form except the $\operatorname{Mat}_r$ example (S2:946). BGS §2.11 also assumes this form for Hilbert series.

**My recommendation.** Decide by whether the main text should allow non-basic $S$.

- **(A) Keep general semisimple $S$ in the main text** (as now: $\operatorname{End}_A(L_j)$ over a division algebra, the $\operatorname{Mat}_r$ example). Keep only the *right* $S$-dual $M^*$, with its graded version and $\theta_r$, in the main text. Move ${}^*N$, ${}^!A$, $\theta_\ell$, the canonical biduality theorem, the transported action and the comparison theorem's proof into advanced blocks. The main text then states $(A^!)^!\cong A$ after fixing $\tau$ (Proposition `prop:quadratic-dual-sides`). This matches your earlier position that the $\Bbbk$-linear dual and the right $S$-dual suffice.
- **(B) Your suggestion: everything $S$-dual becomes advanced.** This is sound **if** the main text restricts to $S=\bigoplus_i\Bbbk e_i$. Define $A^!:=T_S(D(V))/(R^\perp)$ with the blockwise pairing. The $\operatorname{Mat}_r$ example and general semisimple $S$ then move to advanced, and Lectures 4–5 should be checked for uses of ${}^*(A^!_i)$.

I lean towards (A), because the notes currently make an effort to handle general semisimple $S$. If you prefer to teach only basic $S$, (B) is cleaner for students. Once you choose, I will draft it as a separate proposal; it touches S1:962–1351, S2:347–429, the S3 duality subsections and the notation list. It also changes [Replacement 2](#replacement-2), Passages 2–4, which assume the $S$-duals stay in the main text.

**Update after your reply.** You chose to assume $S=\bigoplus_i\Bbbk e_i$ for simplicity; [Replacement 10](#replacement-10) implements this. The $S$-duals stay in the main text. Under the assumption they are canonically identified with $D$ via $\tau_S$, so moving them to advanced later remains an option but is no longer needed.
